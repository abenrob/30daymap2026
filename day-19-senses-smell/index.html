Day 19 · Senses: Smell

France's stinkiest AOP cheeses

Nobody measures how much a cheese smells. So I counted: which protected French cheeses turn up on published "smelliest cheese" lists, and how often.

24 lists, one measured study (Cranfield University, 2004: an electronic nose and 19 human sniffers, 15 cheeses, 11 of them AOP), and the official INAO areas where each cheese may be made. Eight AOP cheeses appear on at least four lists. The darker the area, the more lists name it.

The world's smelliest cheese is not on this map: it has no AOP.

Scroll, stinkiest first.

What the eight have in common

The rind does the work

Five of the eight are washed-rind cheeses: wiped down through ripening with brine, sometimes laced with brandy. The washing feeds the orange-red surface bacteria that make the smell. Cranfield agrees: its smelliest AOP cheese, Pont-l'Évêque, is washed, and so is its overall winner.

The two bloomy rinds, Camembert and Brie, get there on raw milk and time. Roquefort is the only blue, and the smell is in the veins.

Geography helps too. Three of the eight come from Normandy, and their areas are nested: Livarot's sits entirely inside both Camembert's and Pont-l'Évêque's.

The one that got away

Vieux-Boulogne

The cheese Cranfield's nose and panel ranked smelliest of all 15 comes from around Boulogne-sur-Mer and is washed in beer. It was created in the 1980s and has no AOP, so it has no official area: just a dot.

How this was made

Ranking: share of 24 published smelliest-cheese lists that name the cheese, then the position score from the 5 lists that rank by smell, then the Cranfield rank among the 11 AOP cheeses it tested. A consensus of opinion, not a measurement: only Cranfield measured. AOP only; IGP and unprotected cheeses are left out.
