30DayMapChallenge 2026 · Day 26 · Water

The Garonne flood moving downriver, seen through the clouds

In February 2026 the Garonne rose to historic levels, under cloud. Sentinel-1 radar sees through it: calm water reflects the signal away and comes back black. I read 76 Sentinel-1 scenes from ESA's EOPF Sentinel Zarr with xarray-eopf, which calibrates and terrain-corrects each one onto a 50 m IGN grid as it opens. A cell counts as water when it is dark and at least 5 dB darker than a dry autumn reference from the same orbit. The colour is the day open water first appeared there.

Between radar passes the water spreads by height above the river: that motion is interpolated, the dates are observed. Radar misses water between buildings and under trees, so the building count is a floor and measures exposure, not damage. No depth. Copernicus Sentinel-1 data (2025-2026), ESA/Copernicus, via the EOPF Sentinel Zarr samples service.
